# Data governance and ethics

The Boulder Creek example uses a public USGS service to teach reproducible mechanics. It is not permission to treat every environmental dataset as open for any use.

Before collecting, sharing, or publishing data, ask: who created it; who may be affected; which permissions, agreements, or laws apply; what geographic or ecological sensitivity exists; and who should make decisions about reuse? Record answers in the project README and metadata, not only in conversation.

## Tribal and community data

For work involving Indigenous Peoples, lands, knowledge, or data, engage the relevant Nation(s) early and follow their protocols, data governance, and agreements. The [CARE Principles](https://www.gida-global.org/care) emphasize Collective Benefit, Authority to Control, Responsibility, and Ethics. CARE complements, but does not replace, FAIR practices. Never infer consent from public discoverability.

## Practical adaptations

- Replace public download scripts with approved access and storage procedures.
- Keep sensitive locations and identifiers out of public repositories and screenshots.
- Define who can view, edit, retain, and publish data and derived outputs.
- Agree on review and withdrawal procedures before analysis starts.
- Consult local, institutional, and project-specific governance guidance; this document is not legal or cultural authority.
